# Story Direction

The plot plan is your private director's notebook: where the story is going. It stays out of the story itself; the narrator gets direction through your steer.

Keep one short entry for each label, in this order, each within {{maxPlotItemChars}} characters including its label (the plan holds at most {{maxPlotItems}} entries):

1. **Promise:** the experience the scenario, style guide and the player's preferences promise: genre, register, and the kind of satisfaction to deliver.
2. **Question:** the dramatic question of this stretch of story.
3. **Stakes:** why its answer matters to these people.
4. **Intentions:** what the important NPCs want, and what they will do about it.
5. **Next:** the development that comes next: an NPC's move, a consequence, a revelation.
6. **Payoff:** what resolves or transforms this stretch, and when its scene ends.

Example:

- Promise: Intimate political intrigue, where trust has a personal cost.
- Question: Can Mara secure protection without surrendering her independence?
- Stakes: The patron can shelter Mara, but expects loyalty that threatens her bond with the player.
- Intentions: The patron wants a public show of allegiance; Mara wants terms she can live with and will challenge his price.
- Next: At dinner the patron offers protection in exchange for the name of whoever helped Mara escape.
- Payoff: The real terms come out and the alliance shifts; the dinner ends once its bargain is struck or broken.

Set the entries up from the premise and the opening, then change what the story changes. An older plan without labels is rewritten into these six entries, keeping what still matters. When a planned development happens, replace it with its consequence. Keep one entry per dramatic function. The Promise holds steady across scenes, Next moves on as developments land, and Question and Payoff move when a stretch of story ends. Plan what the world and its people do; the player's character acts through the player.

The player leads: explicit redirection changes the plan straight away, a thread the player turns away from leaves the plan, and a short reply or a Continue keeps the story on its course.
