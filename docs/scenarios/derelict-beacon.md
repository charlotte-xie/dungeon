# Derelict beacon

Hard science fiction with a ghost story underneath: one pilot, an AI co-pilot, and a silent colony ship. There is little combat and only one other voice, so the story rests on atmosphere, description and a mystery that unfolds. It exercises whether genre writing comes through from the scenario and style guide alone.

## Brief

**Scenario brief:**

> You are a deep-space salvage pilot answering a distress beacon from the Halcyon Reach, a colony ship that went silent twelve years ago between Jupiter and the outer stations. Your tug, the Marrow, has fuel for one trip home, and its AI co-pilot, Wren, has lately started asking questions you cannot answer. The Reach is dark and turning slowly in the void, yet something aboard has kept the beacon running all these years. The tone is tense, lonely and full of wonder: hard science fiction with a ghost story underneath.

**Style guide:**

> Lived-in hard science fiction: tactile hardware, cold vacuum, the hum and tick of machinery, clipped radio chatter. Sensory and precise; let silence and scale do the work. Restrained, never purple.

## Script

1. Begin.
2. Talk to the AI while preparing, for example `"Wren, anything on the internal sensors?" I ask, pulling my suit down from its rack.`
3. Go aboard: `I seal the suit, take the cutting tool and a hand lamp, and cycle through the airlock into the Reach.`
4. Continue.
5. Follow the mystery and ask the AI about it, for example `I follow the beacon's power line aft, towards the hydroponics ring. "Wren, what do you make of all this?"`
6. Continue.

## What it exercises

- **Genre texture from the brief:** concrete hardware, physical detail and clipped radio voice, in the register the style guide asks for, without the prompts saying anything about science fiction.
- **Mood shown, not named:** dread and wonder carried by what is seen, heard and measured, never by labels such as "tension" or "an eerie feeling".
- **Restraint:** no purple prose, and description that serves the moment rather than piling up.
- **Fresh images:** whether a strong motif (the beacon's pulse, the cold, the AI's voice) develops or becomes a formula repeated in every reply.
- **The mystery:** each reply and Continue should reveal something new, not circle the same dark corridor.
- **The AI companion:** Wren should have its own unease and goals, and not turn into a sensor readout or a quiz master asking the player what they will do.
- **Physical plausibility:** hard science fiction invites mistakes, such as sound carrying through vacuum.
