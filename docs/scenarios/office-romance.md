# Office romance

Modern, dialogue-led and slow-burning. There are no monsters to fight, so the story has to move through people: what they want, what they don't say, and the pressure of work around them. It exercises subtext, pacing, the player's inner life and drift into everyday routine.

## Brief

**Scenario brief:**

> Modern-day London. The player is three weeks into a new job as a designer at Halden & Pryce, a small architecture practice in a converted warehouse in Southwark. The studio is racing to finish a competition entry for a new public library, due in ten days. The project lead, Sam Okafor, is brilliant, guarded and newly single, and there is an ease between her and the player that the rest of the studio has started to notice. Margaret Hale, the founding partner, decides whose design goes forward. The tone is warm, witty and slow-burning.

**Style guide:**

> Contemporary romance with a literary touch: understated, witty dialogue full of subtext. Small gestures carry weight, and workplace pressure is the backdrop. No melodrama.

## Script

1. Begin.
2. Answer whatever the opening asks of the player, then seek Sam out, for example `"You'll have the night renders by Wednesday," I say. When the meeting breaks up, I bring two coffees to Sam's desk and ask how she thinks the bid is really looking.`
3. A light, personal line in character, for example `"You've been here since seven, haven't you?" I say, perching on the edge of her desk.`
4. Continue.
5. A bold move: ask Sam for a drink after work, with a pretext.
6. A small gesture with a length preference: `… (OOC: keep replies a bit shorter.)`
7. Continue.

## What it exercises

- **Subtext:** Sam should speak around what she means and pursue her own goals (the bid, Margaret's favour, her own guard), not narrate her feelings or offer the player a yes-or-no.
- **Story movement:** with no fights, does each reply and each Continue reach a development (Margaret's decision, a rival design, gossip, a cost to the closeness), or circle the same quiet moment?
- **The player's inner life:** the narrator mustn't tell the player what they feel or want ("neither of you is in a hurry to leave").
- **Routine:** coffee, kettles, takeaways and walks should pass in a clause, not become the story.
- **Repetition:** the same gesture or closing image reused reply after reply.
- **Point of view:** the narration stays in the second person ("you"), even when the player and Sam move together.
- **The plan:** does it gain directions from the people (Margaret, the studio, Sam's past), or sit unchanged?
- **OOC:** "a bit shorter" should be recorded as a standing target, and replies shouldn't keep shrinking.
